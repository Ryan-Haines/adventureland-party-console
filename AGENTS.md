# Repository UI guidance

- Right-click context menus, all their submenus, and embedded upgrade previews use opaque WHITE backgrounds with BLACK text and light-gray hover states. This is an explicit exception to the dark dashboard control guidance.


- Keep dashboard controls on dark, opaque backgrounds with explicit high-contrast text and borders.
- Do not rely on the default outline-button colors; they can render as light gray on white in this dashboard.
- For outline, icon, cancel, and secondary-action buttons, set background, text, border, and hover colors explicitly and verify readability against the surrounding panel.

# Coordinator development

- Read [the coordinator README](runtime/coordinator/README.md) before changing coordinator behavior or build/deployment wiring. It maps domains and documents manual validation and activation.
- Edit maintained TypeScript under `runtime/coordinator/`; do not edit generated `.build/runtime/coordinator-*.cjs` bundles or the installed caracAL launcher. The launcher template lives under `tools/caracal/`.
- A service that moves the party or acts for it is a task source: its record reaches the groups and tasks owner (`runtime/tasks/`) through a `task.request` registration that describes the task and decides nothing, and it acts only while `runs` says its task may. Other owners import only `runtime/tasks/api.ts`. Never add a read of another service's state to decide precedence; the rule table (`runtime/tasks/rules.ts`) decides, and `npm run owner:check` fails a cross-owner read or a copy of the table.
- Movement is one owner: every `move`, `smart_move` and `stop` goes out from `runtime/movement/`, its rules live in `runtime/movement/rules.ts`, and other owners reach it through its hooks, `runtime/protocol/` and its two doors, `runtime/movement/api/character.ts` for the character realm and `runtime/movement/api/coordinator.ts` for the coordinator. A group walk confirms delivery by acknowledgement, asks `member.holder` about a member that is not walking, and ends no phase on a round of reports. A member the console already knows is gone (its game socket offline, or its reports stopped past the report-freshness bound) leaves the walk, which reports it and takes it back on its next online report. Read [movement](docs/movement.md) and [group walks](docs/convoy-movement.md) before changing it.
- The status fields the dashboard and the apps read are listed in [the state field registry](docs/state-field-registry.md); [metrics](docs/metrics.md) and [apps](docs/apps.md) describe the telemetry and the key scopes. [Efficiency](docs/efficiency.md) records what each persistence and polling change measured; read it before adding a timer or a write.
- A new saved value is one declaration in the registration of the owner that reads it: the owner's `saved-fields.ts` (`fields.ts` in lifecycle and status), or an add-on's `settings` ([the add-on guide](runtime/addons/README.md)). The settings owner builds every saved, delivered and imported field list from the declarations, so never add a field to a list by hand, and write a setting only through `settings.write`.
- Targeting is one owner on two runtimes: which monster a character takes and whether it may hit it is decided in `runtime/characters/targeting/` (table A's checks in `rules.ts`, the selection pass in `selection.ts`, the gates in `gates.ts`), and the grouped queue in `runtime/coordinator/targeting/`. shared.js builds the module after the doors it reads and hands it those owners' doors (`targetingPorts()`); other owners ask its published reads (`targeting.*`) or import only its two doors, `runtime/characters/targeting/api.ts` and `runtime/coordinator/targeting/api.ts`. Its time values are hidden settings in `targeting/saved-fields.ts`.
- The merchant is three owners: which job runs and every merchant command go through the job owner (`runtime/merchant-jobs/`, its kinds one row each in `rules.ts`, every new job through `jobs.enqueue`, the command slot through `jobs.command`), the merchant's realm through the realm owner (`runtime/merchant-realm/`, `realm.want`), and the merchant's two hands through the hands owner (`runtime/merchant-hands/`, `hands.settle`). Read [realm logistics](docs/realm-logistics.md) before changing the realm owner. Other owners import only their `api.ts` files; a new job kind is one row of the kinds table, never a reason list.
- Inventory, bank and commerce is one owner on two runtimes, `runtime/items/`: an item's fate is one row of the fate table in `runtime/items/rules.ts` (marks, rules, claims, the automatic action), what a job needs is `items.needs`, and storage, production, the markets and mail are `runtime/items/coordinator/storage.ts`, `production.ts`, `market.ts` and `mail.ts`. On a character one operation at a time holds the bag (`partyItems.bag`, `runtime/items/character/owner.ts`) for the kinds `BagOperation` lists, and every reader reads `bag.facts()`, never a flag; the merchant's jobs (a sale, an exchange, mail, a dismantle, a stand or Ponty buy) run one at a time under the merchant jobs owner's runner. Copy identity is `runtime/items/identity.ts`; game limits and picks are in the game-data table of `rules.ts`. Other owners import only `runtime/items/api.ts` or `runtime/items/coordinator/api.ts`, or ask the `items.*` hooks. Read [bank storage](docs/bankboi-storage.md) and [native stand buys](docs/native-stand-buys.md) before changing storage or the stand.
- Events is one owner on two runtimes, `runtime/events/`: what an event is, when its occurrence is due, who may attend, what a member does there and how the group returns are its tables in `runtime/events/rules.ts`; the coordinator half is `runtime/events/coordinator/` and the character half `runtime/events/character/`, each with one install. Other owners import only `runtime/events/api.ts` (`event.presence`, `event.schedule`, the character facts), and a time or count is game data, a measured state or a progress rule, never a pick or a literal. Read [event attendance](docs/events-and-anniversary.md) before changing it.
- Hunts and farming is one owner on two runtimes, `runtime/hunting/`: the Monster Hunt cycle, the farm spot, the combat mode answer, rares and the Phoenix patrol, and the Cave visit are its tables in `runtime/hunting/rules.ts`; the group's farming record has one writer, `coordinator/record.ts`, and every status label comes from rules.ts. The coordinator half is `runtime/hunting/coordinator/` and the character half `runtime/hunting/character/`, each with one install. Other owners import only `runtime/hunting/api.ts` and `runtime/hunting/coordinator/api.ts` or ask its reads (`farming.focus`, `cave.state`, `passive.rule`, `hunt.view`); a time value is a hidden pick in `runtime/hunting/saved-fields.ts` or game data, never a literal. Read [farming combat](docs/farming-combat.md) and [rare hunting](docs/rare-hunting.md) before changing it.
- Turns and cooldowns is one owner: every `attack`, `heal`, `use_skill` and potion of a fighter goes out from `runtime/characters/turns/`, who may spend now is the hold table in `runtime/characters/turns/rules.ts`, and other owners import only `runtime/characters/turns/api.ts` or ask its hooks (`skills.request`, `skills.allows`, `skills.ready`). A spend is a request, never a direct game call. Read [class combat skills](docs/class-combat-skills.md) before changing it, and [escape](docs/escape.md) for the survival owner's escape.
- Building and activating are different operations. Follow the README's supported restart workflow; do not claim a build has reloaded the live coordinator without verifying it. Preserve current character assets when deploying coordinator-only changes.
- After every code or UI change, always produce a completed immutable console artifact before finishing the task. An existing background build may satisfy this requirement, but wait for completion and verify that the candidate contains the final changes; pushing commits or starting a build alone is insufficient.
- Do not deploy or activate an artifact, or restart the running console, unless the user explicitly instructs you to do so for that action. By default, leave the completed candidate staged for the user to load through the Party Console. Report the staged artifact and distinguish it from the active build.

# Add-ons

- A new feature starts as an add-on in `runtime/addons/<name>/`, and a bug fix goes into the owner that decides wrongly. Read [the add-on guide](runtime/addons/README.md) first.
- Answer only hooks the published list [runtime/addons/HOOKS.md](runtime/addons/HOOKS.md) names, and import the core only through `runtime/hooks/addon-api.ts`; never call the game from an add-on. Run `npm run addon:check` before calling an add-on done.
- A new hook, event or slot is one row in its owner's `runtime/hooks/rows/<NN>-<area>.ts` with `since`, `fixture` and, on a request, `addonMayAsk`; a published type is one line in its owner's `runtime/hooks/api/<NN>-<area>.ts`. Regenerate the list with `node runtime/hooks/cli/hook-list.mts`.

# Game TypeScript types

- Prefer `typed-adventureland` types when describing the same game object. Use `Pick`, `Partial`, or indexed access for subsets instead of repeating compatible fields.
- Extend upstream types for additional fields. When field semantics differ, use `Omit` plus explicit replacements; document why broader IDs, optional fields, or normalized values are needed.
- Keep application protocols, jobs, and intentionally different external payloads local. Do not force partial wire data into complete game-object types.
- Document verified upstream gaps alongside compatibility extensions and review them when upgrading the package. Do not add casts solely to hide incompatible contracts.

# Testing

- For rising game ping or runtime slowdown, read [performance investigation lessons](docs/performance-investigations.md). Verify local callback delay and growing history work before changing cooldown compensation.

- Always run `npm run typecheck` after implementation changes and fix compiler errors before handing the work back. Leaving gameplay verification and E2E testing to the user does not skip this check.
- Run `npm run owner:check` after changing a game call, timer, interval, event handler or socket listener, or an import between owner modules. It fails on one outside its owner that `tools/owner-check/listed-rows.json` does not list, so a new row goes into that file with its owner first. It reads only the repository: `listed-rows.json` and `ownership-map.json` in `tools/owner-check/`. `node tools/owner-check.mjs --map-out tools/owner-check/ownership-map.json` regenerates the map after a deliberate move of rows.
- Never write unit tests after you write code.
- Highly prefer E2E tests as the sole testing mechanism. Use them to verify complex features work. At the end of E2E tests, produce a verifiable and repeatable artifact.
- All gameplay E2Es must use ChronAL virtual time by default so gameplay waits do not run at real-time speed. Preserve browser, deployment, persistence, and telemetry coverage and existing behavioral assertions when integrating ChronAL; migrating a subset or retaining scenario counts alone does not satisfy this requirement. Validate runtime improvements locally with comparable runs and retain repeatable timing artifacts. Keep the native backend available for explicit parity checks.
- Remove owned per-scenario E2E workspaces after owned processes stop and report attachments finish copying. Preserve verifiable reports and timing evidence; retain writable workspaces only for explicit diagnostics or failed attachment copies. Do not accumulate copies of staged CODE/assets across completed tests.
- If you must test a system in isolation, first write down all the ways it could fail, then write the code.
- Treat native action completion, queue removal, client persistence, and coordinator/dashboard telemetry as separate asynchronous milestones. Completing one does not prove the others have arrived. Before asserting asynchronously published state, use a bounded poll for the exact expected values from one fresh snapshot; retain strict counts and ownership checks rather than adding sleeps, widening expectations, or blindly increasing timeouts.
- When investigating timing failures, compare native receipts, client storage, and later coordinator observations before changing gameplay code. Preserve timestamped observations on failure so delayed publication can be distinguished from lost, duplicated, or misattributed results. Fixture fault injection must target the current operation and must not itself stall unrelated status or movement requests.

Run `npm test` for real-game and console E2E and inspect `.build/e2e-report/` and `.build/e2e-results/`.
See [the testing guide](docs/testing.md) for setup, artifacts, fixture boundaries, and
the retained isolated regression exceptions. Do not replace observable behavior
checks with source-text, callback-order, or CSS-class assertions.
